## Verified Historical & Physics Facts
- **102-Day Prototype (1940):** In April 1940, the British Purchasing Commission asked North American Aviation (Inglewood, California) to license-build Curtiss P-40s. Instead, NAA president "Dutch" Kindelberger and chief designer Edgar Schmued designed the NA-73X from scratch; the airframe rolled out on 9 September 1940 (**102 days** after signing) and first flew on 26 October 1940 (117 days). *(Sources: Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum; Wikipedia — North American P-51 Mustang)*
- **High-Altitude Failure & Photo-Recon Demotion (1941–1942):** Early Mustang Mk I aircraft used the Allison V-1710-39 engine with only a single-stage supercharger, losing power rapidly above 12,000–15,000 ft. Unable to fight Luftwaffe fighters at bomber altitudes, the RAF fitted oblique F.24 aerial cameras behind the pilot's seat and relegated it to low-altitude tactical reconnaissance over fields, roads, and bridges. *(Sources: Imperial War Museums; Airscape Magazine)*
- **Ronald Harker's 30-Minute Flight & Merlin Swap (April–October 1942):** On 30 April 1942, Rolls-Royce test pilot Ronald "Ronnie" Harker flew a Mustang Mk I at RAF Duxford for ~30 minutes and proposed bolting the **Rolls-Royce Merlin 61** (two-speed, two-stage intercooled supercharger) into the Mustang's low-drag laminar-flow airframe. First flown as the Mustang Mk X in October 1942 (and produced in America with the license-built Packard V-1650 Merlin as P-51B/C/D), top speed jumped from ~390 mph to **~440 mph (~708 km/h)** above 25,000 ft. *(Sources: Rolls-Royce Heritage Trust; Vintage Aviation News)*
- **Physical Mechanisms (Two-Stage Supercharger + Meredith Effect):**
  1. **Two-stage supercharger:** Compresses thin upper-atmosphere air in two consecutive stages (with an aftercooler preventing detonation) so the V12 burns fuel with sea-level oxygen density at 25,000–30,000 ft.
  2. **Meredith Effect belly scoop:** Proved by F.W. Meredith (RAE, 1935) and integrated into the P-51's aft ventral radiator duct—incoming air slows in a diffuser, expands across hot glycol/oil radiator cores, and accelerates out a narrowing rear nozzle, canceling cooling drag above ~300 mph and adding slight net thrust at ~400+ mph. *(Sources: RAE Report R&M 1683, F.W. Meredith 1935; Kitplanes)*
- **Berlin Escort & Göring Payoff (March 1944):** With laminar-flow efficiency and underwing drop tanks, P-51s escorted USAAF B-17/B-24 bombers all the way to Berlin and back. Luftwaffe commander Hermann Göring later admitted that when he saw Mustangs flying over Berlin, he knew the war was lost (*"I knew the jig was up"*). *(Source: U.S. Air Force National Museum / Adolf Galland memoirs)*

Pesawat tempur terbaik di Perang Dunia Kedua awalnya diparkir di bawah awan cuma buat memotret jalanan, karena mesinnya gampang bengek.
Tahun 1940, pabrik kecil di California merakit P-51 Mustang dari kertas kosong hanya dalam seratus dua hari—kebanggaan baru Sekutu.
Di dekat tanah, larinya kencang sampai menerbangkan topi para jenderal di landasan.
Tapi begitu pilot menanjak ke langit lima kilometer tempat pertempuran sesungguhnya, mesin Allison bawaannya langsung batuk-batuk dan kehilangan tenaga.
Pesawat Jerman melesat santai melewati mereka di atas awan, sementara Mustang dihukum terbang rendah memotret jalanan dan ladang sapi.
Untungnya, bodi Mustang membelah angin paling licin di dunia.
Sialnya, bodi secepat peluru itu dikasih paru-paru sebesar burung pipit.
Sampai April 1942, pilot uji Inggris bernama Ronnie Harker menjajalnya tiga puluh menit, lalu menyuruh mekanik mencopot mesin aslinya dan memasang mesin Rolls-Royce Merlin.
Masalahnya, di langit tinggi udaranya sangat tipis, jadi bensin susah terbakar.
Pernah meniup bara arang pakai pompa tangan? Tiupan pelan cuma bikin berasap, tapi kalau udaranya dipadatkan dua tahap beruntun, baranya langsung menyembur.
Supercharger dua tingkat milik Merlin memeras udara tipis di atas awan sampai sepadat udara pantai—sementara corong radiator di perutnya membuang udara panas ke belakang seperti jet kecil.
Seketika larinya menembus tujuh ratus kilometer per jam, dan sanggup mengawal pesawat pengebom jauh sampai ke atas kota Berlin.
Panglima udara Jerman Hermann Göring pun sadar perang sudah tamat saat melihat Mustang di langit Berlin—pesawat yang tadinya cuma disuruh memotret ladang.

## Optional English Script (~222 words · target ~60 seconds @ 3.7 words/s)

The deadliest fighter plane of World War Two started out taking pictures of cows because it couldn't breathe above the clouds.
It's 1940, and a small California factory builds the brand-new P-51 Mustang from a blank sheet of paper in just a hundred and two days.
Down near the grass, it screams past the hangar fast enough to blow every general's hat into the bushes.
Then pilots climb five miles up where the real air war happens, and its American Allison engine starts coughing like an old lawnmower.
German fighters wave as they zoom overhead, so the army straps a camera behind the seat and sends their wonder-plane down into the mud to photograph French roads.
Luckily, the Mustang had the slickest body ever put in the sky.
Unluckily, it had the lungs of a sparrow.
In April 1942, a British test pilot named Ronnie Harker flies one for thirty minutes, climbs out, and says: bolt a Rolls-Royce Merlin engine into that nose.
You see, five miles up, the air is too thin to burn fuel properly.
Ever pumped air onto dying campfire coals with bellows? One lazy puff just makes smoke, but squeeze two big gulps in a row and the fire roars.
Merlin's two-stage supercharger crammed thin sky air down to sea-level thickness, while the scoop under the Mustang's belly turned hot radiator air into a tiny jet push.
Overnight its speed jumped past four hundred and forty miles an hour, escorting bombers all the way to Berlin.
When Germany's air chief saw Mustangs over his capital, he said he knew the war was lost—to the plane they once sent out to photograph cows.
