## Verified Historical & Physics Facts
- **$3 Billion Program vs $1.9 Billion Manhattan Project (1940–1945):** The Boeing B-29 Superfortress program cost roughly **$3 billion**—over a billion dollars more than the $1.9 billion Manhattan Project that developed the atomic bomb. It introduced fully **pressurized front and rear crew compartments** connected by a long cylindrical crawl-tunnel over the bomb bays, plus remote-controlled analog computer gun turrets. *(Sources: National Museum of the United States Air Force; Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum)*
- **Magnesium Crankcase Fires in the Wright R-3350 Duplex-Cyclone (1943–1944):** Each B-29 was powered by four 18-cylinder twin-row **Wright R-3350 Duplex-Cyclone** radial engines (2,200 hp each) tightly cowled to minimize aerodynamic drag. To save weight, the crankcases and accessory housings were cast from **magnesium alloy**. During heavy climbs, airflow failed to reach the rear row of cylinders, causing exhaust valves to overheat, fail, and ignite oil and the magnesium metal itself—burning at ~5,000 °F like a giant white flare (famously destroying the second XB-29 prototype in Seattle on February 18, 1943). *(Sources: Smithsonian Air & Space Magazine; NASA / NACA Wartime Engine Cooling Reports)*
- **Physical Mechanism (Curved Cooling Baffles + Cowl Vents):**
  - Even in sub-zero high-altitude air, tightly cowled twin-row radial engines suffered boundary-layer separation: incoming air skimmed past the front cylinders without wrapping around the rear cylinder fins, while closed cowl flaps trapped a pocket of stagnant superheated air.
  - **Fix:** Engineers installed **curved aluminum inter-cylinder baffles** (deflectors) that scooped and channeled incoming air directly around the rear cylinder heads and valve guides, paired with revised cowl-flap vents and direct fuel injection.
- **Soviet Tu-4 Bolt-for-Bolt Reverse Engineering (1944–1947):** After three damaged USAAF B-29s (*Ramp Tramp*, *Ding Hao!*, and *General H.H. Arnold Special*) made emergency landings at Vladivostok in 1944, Joseph Stalin interned them and ordered Andrei Tupolev's bureau to reverse-engineer the bomber **bolt for bolt** as the **Tupolev Tu-4**. Because Stalin forbade any unauthorized changes, Soviet engineers famously replicated an accidental factory drill hole and repair patch found in the wing of one of the Boeing aircraft. *(Sources: Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum — Tupolev Tu-4; Duffy & Kandalov, Tupolev: The Man and His Aircraft)*

## English Script (~262 words · target ~64 seconds @ 4.0 words/s)

During World War Two, America spent three billion dollars on a wonder-bomber whose engines were accidentally made out of fireworks.
It's nineteen forty-three, and Boeing rolls out the B-twenty-nine Superfortress, a flying spaceship that costs a billion dollars more than the atomic bomb itself.
While other bomber crews freeze in fleece jackets, B-twenty-nine flyers sit in warm pressurized cabins and crawl from nose to tail through a cozy metal tunnel.
But to make the wings extra sleek, engineers crammed four massive engines into tight cowlings and cast their parts from lightweight magnesium.
The moment pilots climb with a heavy load, the back cylinders starve for air and ignite the magnesium block like a giant roadside flare.
Luckily, the B-twenty-nine could fly higher than almost any fighter in the sky.
Unluckily, its own wings kept celebrating the Fourth of July.
You see, even in freezing air, an engine overheats if the wind can't reach the back row.
Ever blown across a deep bowl of boiling soup? A flat puff just skims the surface while the bottom stays scalding, until you angle a spoon to scoop the breeze straight down inside.
Mechanics bolted curved metal baffles and wide cowl vents into every nacelle, scooping cold sky air right around the rear cylinders so the engines stayed cool.
Soon fleets of silver Superfortresses ruled the Pacific, until three damaged planes made emergency landings in the Soviet Union.
Stalin ordered his engineers to copy the bomber bolt for bolt, so every Soviet clone rolled out with a tiny patch over the exact same accidental drill hole a Boeing worker made in Seattle.
